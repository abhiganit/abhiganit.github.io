Eight problems
between us and space.

Free picture books for small children, and for whoever is reading to them. Each one takes a single problem that stood between people and the sky, and the trick that got past it. Print them at home.

The books

Sixteen pages each, meant to be read aloud. The pictures carry the idea; the last two pages are for the grown-up — the real history and the numbers, so you are not improvising. Read them in order: each one ends on the problem the next one solves.

Print one

Two versions of every book. The reading PDF is sixteen plain pages. The booklet PDF is four sheets that fold into a real book — that is the one worth the trouble.

Paper

  • Ordinary 20 lb copy paper will not survive a four-year-old. Use 32 lb (120 gsm) for the pages.
  • 65 lb card for the cover, fed one sheet at a time.
  • Matte, never glossy — it smears with inkjet and is harder to read at bedtime.
  • Nothing on these pages is a solid dark fill, so a whole book costs very little ink.

Printing

  • Print the booklet PDF double-sided, flipping on the short edge.
  • Run two sheets first to check which way your printer flips. This is the one setting people get wrong.

Folding and stitching

  • Stack all four sheets flat, then fold once through the whole stack. Don't fold them one at a time.
  • Check the page numbers run 2, 3, 4, 5 as you turn. If the covers are inside, flip the stack and refold.
  • Open flat at the middle. Three faint marks on the fold show where to pierce.
  • Sew: out through the middle, in at the top, down and out at the bottom, back in at the middle. Tie around the long stitch and trim.

Play

Three ideas from the books that a printed page genuinely cannot do, because they only make sense once something moves. Everything here runs real physics — the curves are calculated, not drawn.

Falling and missing

From Book Four. Fire the cannon harder and harder until the ball stops landing.

Too heavy to lift

From Book Five. The same fuel every time. The only choice is how many pieces to split it into.

Push against nothing

From Book Three. Nothing is touching the ground. Throw a ball and see what happens to you.

Made for one small boy who likes rockets. Free to print, copy, and hand to anyone else's children.
