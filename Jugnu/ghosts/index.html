A ghost who has
never scared anybody.

Two hundred years in the same house, one trick at a time, and not a single success. A picture book for small children, and for whoever is reading it to them. Read it on a screen or print it at home.

The book

Sixteen pages, meant to be read aloud. Bartholomew tries the moan, the wall, the cold, and appearing suddenly — and fails at every one of them, alone, in order. Then he works out that he has been doing them one at a time.

Bartholomew

Who has haunted this house for two hundred years.

Nobody has ever heard him. Nobody ever does — except the cat, who has seen him all along and is not impressed by anything at all.

Print it

Two versions of the same sixteen pages. The screen version has a cream page and is the smaller download. The printing version puts everything on white paper, which is the one that will not drink your ink.

Paper

  • 32 lb (120 gsm) paper if you want it to survive a four-year-old. Ordinary 20 lb will do for a first try.
  • Matte, never glossy. Glossy smears with inkjet and catches the lamp at bedtime.
  • The pictures are full colour and go to the edge of their panels, so this book costs more ink than the space ones. That is the trade for the pictures.

Printing

  • Sixteen plain pages, in order. Nothing is imposed and nothing needs folding.
  • Single-sided is simplest and lies flattest. Double-sided halves the paper — flip on the long edge so the pages stay upright.
  • Print at 100%, not "fit to page", or the margins will creep.

Holding it together

  • Two staples down the left edge is enough, and a four-year-old can turn the pages without help.
  • A cheap slide binder or three hole-punches and a treasury tag all work. Nothing here needs sewing.
  • Print the cover on 65 lb card, fed one sheet at a time, if you want it to last.

Reading it

  • The line that repeats is Bartholomew tried again. Let whoever you are reading to say it.
  • The cat is on almost every page. Finding it is most of the fun.

Make your own

Two printables drawn in Jugnu's own hand rather than the book's, because a sheet ghost is a dome, a scalloped hem and one eye — which is exactly the sort of thing this site draws. They cost almost no ink.

How to Scare People

A handbook for new ghosts, by Bartholomew.

Four lessons with diagrams, a difficulty rating, and an honest note at the bottom of each one about how it went. Then the fifth lesson, which is the whole trick. Ends with a page to design your own ghost and tick which powers it has, and a licence with a space to draw yourself.
